KOL Profiling Engine for an IBD Advisory Board

Brief: Profiled 51 inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's and UC) key opinion leaders across US and ex-US markets for a pharma client's advisory board and recommended the board's strategic discussion themes. Delivered in 15 days against a two-month manual baseline, and the workflow is now being built into Lucid's Fusion platform as a reusable, asset-tailored KOL profiling capability.

Situation
Inflammatory bowel disease covers two chronic, relapsing conditions of the gut, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. In genetically susceptible people, an exaggerated immune response to the gut's own microbiome drives persistent inflammation: in UC it is continuous and confined to the lining of the colon; in Crohn's it is patchy, can affect any part of the digestive tract and goes through the full bowel wall, leading to strictures and fistulas. Onset is typically in the 20s and 30s, there is no cure, and around seven million people live with it worldwide, so treatment is about inducing and holding remission for decades. That makes IBD one of the most crowded spaces in immunology: anti-TNFs going biosimilar, IL-23 inhibitors fighting for first-line position, oral JAK and S1P drugs, and new mechanisms such as TL1A in phase 3. With no reliable biomarker to predict response, the live debates among gastroenterologists are about sequencing, how early and aggressively to treat, and safety trade-offs between classes. The client needed to know not just who the experts were, but where each stood on those debates and who would shape the conversation about this asset.
Complication
The data existed but was scattered. H1 gave the clinical and publication backbone; congress presence (ECCO, DDW, UEG Week), social media activity and recent positions sat across LinkedIn, X and journal sites. Done manually for 51 people this was a two-month job, with inconsistent output because every analyst summarises an expert differently. And the client wanted an agenda, not a directory, which meant synthesising across 51 people rather than describing them one at a time.
Solution
  1. Designed a standard profile template so every KOL was captured against the same dimensions: clinical focus, institutional role, publication themes, congress activity, digital voice and stated positions relevant to the asset and indication.
  2. Pulled core profiles and publications from H1, then used Claude Cowork to extract and structure social media and congress activity against the template, so the work became quality control and judgement rather than reading 51 timelines by hand.
  3. Synthesised across all 51 profiles to surface recurring themes, points of disagreement and emerging topics, and translated them into proposed strategic discussion themes for the advisory board, each mapped to the KOLs most likely to drive it.
Impact
Delivered in 15 days of effort against a two-month baseline. The client received 51 consistent, evidence-backed profiles and an advisory board agenda grounded in what these experts are actually saying, rather than what we assumed they would say.

In-licensing Due Diligence · Immunology · Ongoing strategic support

Asset Due Diligence: Bispecific Antibodies in Immunology

Brief: Produced due-diligence decks on bispecific antibody assets pairing TL1A with IL-17 and with IL-6, for a biotech client building its pipeline through in-licensing, to whom we provide ongoing strategic support on asset evaluation. Each deck covered mechanism, target biology, clinically proven indications, efficacy and safety benchmarks, study-design comparison, a recommendation on viable indications, and a deep-dive on every approved and discontinued asset against each target. The diligence supported the client's decision to move forward: non-binding term sheets were signed to in-license an IL-17A/F × TNF asset and an IL-17A × TL1A asset, and the client has since closed its Series B.

Situation
TL1A (TNF-like ligand 1A) is one of the most sought-after new targets in immunology. It sits upstream of both inflammation and fibrosis, which is why anti-TL1A antibodies are in late-stage trials for ulcerative colitis and Crohn's and are being explored in fibrotic disease. The field has moved quickly from single-target antibodies to bispecifics that pair TL1A with a second validated axis. IL-17 is proven in psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and axial spondyloarthritis; IL-6 is proven in rheumatoid arthritis, giant cell arteritis and cytokine release syndrome; TNF is the original immunology blockbuster class, now largely biosimilar. The client, a biotech that builds its pipeline by in-licensing rather than discovering in-house, had identified bispecific assets combining TL1A with these pathways and needed to know, before committing to a term sheet, whether each combination was a credible thesis and in which indications it could win.
Complication
Neither combination has a clinical precedent, so the case had to be built from the component parts, and the parts pull in different directions. IL-17 inhibitors have failed or worsened disease in Crohn's, which is TL1A's lead indication, so the strongest TL1A data and the strongest IL-17 data do not overlap cleanly. IL-6 blockade carries class safety signals in the gut that have derailed earlier IBD programmes. Efficacy data across the three targets comes from trials with different endpoints, populations and durations, so a like-for-like benchmark did not exist. And the history of discontinued assets against each target was scattered across trial registries, conference abstracts and press releases.
Solution
  1. Mapped the mechanism of action and target biology for TL1A, IL-17 and IL-6, including where the pathways intersect and where blocking one could undermine the other.
  2. Built an indication-by-target matrix: where each pathway is clinically proven, where it has failed, and where the evidence is still open.
  3. Normalised efficacy and safety benchmarks across pivotal trials and compared study designs (endpoints, populations, induction and maintenance structure) so the client could read results like-for-like.
  4. Profiled every approved and discontinued asset against each target, with the stated reason for each discontinuation.
  5. Recommended the indications where a dual-target rationale is strongest and flagged those where the component evidence argues against it.
Impact
The client moved forward on both fronts. Non-binding term sheets were signed to in-license two assets, an IL-17A/F × TNF bispecific and an IL-17A × TL1A bispecific, and the client has since closed its Series B financing with those assets in the pipeline story. The indication-by-target evidence map and the normalised benchmark structure are now the standing framework for the client's subsequent asset reviews, and the engagement continues as ongoing strategic support.
Full-stack · Med Comms

MoA Image Generator

A full-stack tool for Med Comms teams to generate Mechanism of Action (MoA) scientific illustrations for pharmaceutical assets — combining PubMed research, LLM prompt engineering, and image generation in one workflow.

  1. Enter a pharmaceutical asset name (e.g. semaglutide, pembrolizumab).
  2. The app queries PubMed for relevant MoA steps.
  3. Select an LLM (Anthropic / OpenAI / Gemini) to write a structured image prompt.
  4. Review and edit the prompt before image generation.
  5. Select an image generation model (DALL·E 3 / Imagen 3) and generate 2–3 images.
