Disease contextTap to open: what IBD is and how it is treated
What IBD is
  • Two chronic, relapsing gut conditions: Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis (UC).
  • Driven by an exaggerated immune response to the gut microbiome in genetically susceptible people.
  • Onset typically in the 20s–30s; no cure; ~7 million patients worldwide; goal is to induce and hold remission for decades.
Crohn's vs. UC
  • UC: continuous inflammation confined to the colon lining; colectomy is curative but life-altering.
  • Crohn's: patchy, anywhere mouth to anus, through the full bowel wall; strictures, fistulas and abscesses; surgery common but not curative.
  • UC is usually the proof-of-concept indication for a new mechanism; Crohn's follows 1–2 years later.
Treatment landscape
  • Base: 5-ASAs, steroids, immunomodulators.
  • Advanced: anti-TNFs (now biosimilar), anti-integrin vedolizumab, IL-23 inhibitors (current growth engine), oral JAK and S1P drugs.
  • Pipeline: anti-TL1A antibodies and obefazimod in phase 3.
  • Most monotherapies plateau at 30–40% remission, which drives the sequencing and combination debates.

Asset Due Diligence

Brief: Evaluated two early-stage bispecific antibody assets in immunology, each combining two validated inflammatory pathways, for a biotech client building its pipeline through in-licensing. Client signed non-binding term sheets on both assets and has since closed its Series B.

Open the caseClose
Situation
  • Biotech client that builds its pipeline by in-licensing rather than in-house discovery; we provide ongoing strategic support on asset evaluation.
  • Two early-stage bispecifics identified, each pairing a clinically validated cytokine pathway with a second, aimed at chronic inflammatory indications where single-target biologics have hit a response ceiling.
  • Dual-pathway bispecifics are the industry's bet on moving that ceiling, but very few have reached the market.
  • Before a term sheet, client needed: scientific credibility, differentiation vs. incumbents and competitors, and the indications where each asset could win.
Complication
  • Early-stage assets, so the case had to be built from component evidence.
  • Asset one: both pathways proven in the target indications, but poor precedent; earlier bispecifics on the same pair stalled at phase 2, one for failing to beat the standard of care, one on safety.
  • Asset two: attractive biology, but one of its two targets had only been validated clinically in a different indication; no human data in the target indications.
  • Class safety signals from each pathway had to be weighed against the target populations, and dual blockade has historically added infection risk without adding efficacy.
  • Dense competition: a dual-pathway antibody already approved in both target indications, several late-stage programmes, and big-pharma programmes on one of the targets that could expand into the same indications.
  • No like-for-like benchmark: pivotal trials used different endpoints, populations and durations.
Solution
  1. Mapped MoA and target biology for the three pathways involved, including where they converge or could undermine each other.
  2. Built an indication-by-target evidence matrix: proven, failed, open, across the target and adjacent inflammatory indications.
  3. Assessed scientific differentiation vs. incumbents; mapped every competitor programme by target, modality and stage.
  4. Normalised efficacy and safety benchmarks across pivotal trials and compared study designs so results read like-for-like.
  5. Reviewed class and combination safety signals, using discontinued dual-cytokine programmes as precedents.
  6. Profiled every approved and discontinued asset per target, with reasons for discontinuation.
  7. Recommended where each asset's dual-target rationale is strongest and what it must demonstrate to be differentiated.
Impact
  • Client moved forward on both assets: non-binding term sheets signed to in-license both bispecifics.
  • Client has since closed its Series B with those assets in the pipeline story.
  • Evidence matrix, competitor map and benchmark structure now the standing framework for the client's subsequent asset reviews; engagement continues.
Market contextTap to open: the immunology market and why bispecifics
Why bispecifics, why now
  • Immunology is the largest therapeutic market after oncology.
  • Anti-TNFs that built the market are now biosimilar; growth comes from IL-17, IL-23 and oral JAKs.
  • Bispecifics that block two cytokines at once are the next wave, aimed at a response ceiling single-target drugs have not moved in a decade.
What a bispecific is
  • An antibody engineered to bind two targets at once, so one molecule can block two inflammatory pathways that a patient's disease runs on.
  • The promise is higher response rates than either single-target drug; the risk is additive side effects, above all infection, from blocking two arms of the immune system.
Why due diligence is hard here
  • Early-stage bispecifics rarely have their own clinical data, so the case rests on each component pathway's track record in the target indication.
  • Pathways do not transfer between indications automatically: a mechanism that works in one inflammatory disease has failed outright in another.
  • Several dual-cytokine programmes have been discontinued at phase 2, so precedent matters as much as biology.

Situation
  • Client with a portfolio spanning several Alzheimer's modalities, diagnostic and therapeutic, planning its US market entry.

  • The client needed an outside-in view: how patients actually travel through the system, where each modality would slot in, who the patients are, and how large the realistic US opportunity was.

  • Published epidemiology counts prevalence, not the funnel: who presents, who gets screened, who is referred and who reaches confirmation and treatment are all different, smaller numbers.
  • Patient voice is scattered across forums, social media and advocacy groups, with caregivers often speaking for patients.
  • The market was moving as we worked: new diagnostic modalities were reaching FDA clearance and guidelines on their use were still forming, so the sizing had to hold under several adoption scenarios rather than one.

  4. Ran scenario analysis on the levers the client could influence or needed to plan for: primary-care vs. specialist adoption, reimbursement timing, and uptake of anti-amyloid therapy.
  5. Delivered the ecosystem map and the funnel model as the evidence base for the client's market access strategy.
Impact
  • Client received a single, sourced view of the disease ecosystem: where patients are lost, where each modality adds the most value, and who the realistic early adopters are.

  • Client adopted the AI-enabled persona approach as its method for patient insight.

Disease & market contextTap to open: Alzheimer's, diagnosis and the US market
Alzheimer's disease
  • Progressive neurodegenerative disease; the most common cause of dementia.
  • Defined biologically by amyloid plaques and tau tangles, which build up 10–20 years before symptoms.
  • ~7.4 million Americans aged 65+ live with Alzheimer's dementia (2026); many more are in the pre-symptomatic or mild cognitive impairment (MCI) stages.
Why the pathway is changing
  • Anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab 2023, donanemab 2024) require confirmed amyloid pathology before treatment, making diagnosis a gate to therapy.
  • Confirmation has relied on amyloid PET (costly, limited scanner capacity) or CSF via lumbar puncture (invasive).
  • New modalities are arriving at every step: blood-based biomarker tests (first FDA-cleared in 2025), digital cognitive assessments, and subcutaneous and next-generation therapies.
US market signals
  • Global blood-based Alzheimer's biomarker diagnostics market, one of the fastest-moving segments: ~$170M in 2025, projected to reach ~$840M by 2035 (~17% CAGR), with North America holding ~40% share.
  • Biomarker testing among Americans 55+ grew 37.5x between 2023 and 2025 as new tests were cleared.
  • 17% of newly diagnosed Alzheimer's patients had a biomarker test within six months of diagnosis in 2025, up from under 1% in 2023.
  • Diagnoses are shifting earlier: MCI diagnosis rates more than doubled between 2020 and 2025.

Sources: Towards Healthcare (2026); Trilliant Health (2026).

Domain contextTap to open: what MoA visuals are and why AI struggles with them
What an MoA visual is
  • Illustrates how a drug works at the molecular or cellular level: target bound, pathway blocked or activated, downstream effect on disease.
  • Used across MSL decks, congress posters, HCP education and internal training.
  • Must survive medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review before use.
Why they cost so much
  • Commissioned from specialist medical illustrators, briefed by a medical writer, revised over several rounds with scientific reviewers.
  • Cost is driven by accuracy and review burden, not drawing: a single image can run to $20K and several weeks.
Why generative AI struggles
  • Models are trained on general imagery, not validated biology: plausible but incorrect receptors, pathways and cell structures.
  • Historically poor text-label rendering; hard to keep a consistent style across a series.
  • Usable output depends on structured prompting plus a review step, so tool choice, a prompt library and training matter as much as the model.

Domain contextTap to open: SOPs, GCP and sponsor oversight
Quality management in clinical development
  • A sponsor's quality management system (QMS) is the set of policies, SOPs and records that show trials are run to protocol and to Good Clinical Practice (GCP).
  • SOPs are the operating layer: how a study is started, how data are monitored, how vendors are overseen, how a study is closed.
  • Inspectors (FDA, EMA, MHRA) audit against the sponsor's own SOPs, so a contradictory SOP set is a finding waiting to happen.
Sponsor vs. CRO
  • Sponsors can outsource trial activities to CROs, but under ICH GCP the sponsor keeps ultimate responsibility for data quality and participant safety.
  • That means the sponsor must document oversight of CRO work, not just receive it, which is where duplicated records and unclear roles cause the most trouble.
Why SOPs are being rewritten now
  • ICH E6(R3), the revised GCP guideline finalised in 2025, puts risk-based quality management, proportionality and sponsor oversight at the centre.
  • Industry is moving from "monitor everything" to risk-based quality management (RBQM), which changes how data monitoring and oversight SOPs are written.

Task Mining Tool Selection for Procurement

Brief: Ran the technology assessment for a large mining company looking to find automation opportunities in its procurement function: scanned emerging task-mining technologies, drafted the RFI, ran vendor demos and a structured evaluation, and recommended Soroco for its ability to map how work is actually done and quantify the automation gains. Open the caseClose
Situation
  • Global mining company with a large, transaction-heavy procurement function spread across regions and systems.
  • Leadership believed there were efficiency and automation gains in source-to-pay, but had no objective picture of how the work was actually performed day to day.
  • Task-mining tools, which record and analyse desktop activity to reconstruct real workflows, had matured enough to be considered; the client asked Accenture to evaluate the market and recommend one.

Domain contextTap to open: task mining vs. process mining, and procurement
Task mining vs. process mining
  • Process mining reads system event logs (ERP, CRM) to reconstruct how a process flows between systems.
  • Task mining records user desktop activity (clicks, keystrokes, application switches) to see the manual work that happens between and inside those systems: the spreadsheets, emails and re-keying that logs never capture.
  • Together they show both the official process and the real one; task mining is where most automation candidates hide.
Why procurement
  • Source-to-pay (requisition, sourcing, purchase order, receipt, invoice, payment) is high-volume, rules-based and spread across many hands, which makes it a classic target for automation.
  • In mining, procurement also carries heavy spend on equipment and contractors across remote sites, so small efficiency gains compound.
RFI vs. RFP
  • An RFI (request for information) asks vendors what they can do and narrows a longlist; an RFP (request for proposal) asks shortlisted vendors for a priced, scoped offer.
  • Getting the RFI criteria right is where most of the selection is decided.

Domain contextTap to open: archival, retention and why it is harder than it sounds
What a case management system holds
  • Structured records of individual cases (incidents, claims, grievances, investigations) plus their documents, correspondence and audit trail.
  • In a heavy-industry company these records can carry legal, safety and regulatory weight for years after a case closes.
Why archive from a live CRM
  • CRM platforms such as Microsoft Dynamics charge for storage and slow down as history accumulates; keeping 15 years of closed cases in production costs money and performance.
  • Retention schedules and legal holds still require old records to be kept intact and retrievable, so the answer is an archive, not deletion.
  • An archive has to preserve not just the data but its context: metadata, relationships between records, and the evidence that nothing was changed.
What "clean and compliant" means
  • Clean: profiled, de-duplicated, consistently formatted data with reconciled record counts.
  • Compliant: retention rules applied, access controls in place, and an audit trail of the migration itself.
  • The project-management challenge is less technical than organisational: getting legal, IT, records and the business to agree and sign off together.
